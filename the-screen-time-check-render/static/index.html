What this check is

Four areas, eleven scored answers, one number

It scores what your screens are doing right now, names every gap in your own words, and matches each one to the part of OneRevel that would close it. It is a check, not a quote. No prices appear anywhere in it.

1

What is on them

When the message last changed, who decides what goes up, and how much of the estate has not moved in a month.

2

How fast it changes

From deciding something to seeing it on screen. How many people it takes, and whether anything changes by itself from data.

3

What it is up against

A pro team CIO told Brian his competition is not other teams, it is your living room. This asks whether your screens beat the phone in someone's pocket.

4

What you can show for it

Whether a dark screen gets noticed, and whether you could prove to a sponsor, a brand team or a boss that a message ran.

How this sits beside what OneRevel already publishes. Their seven phase Why Revel framework describes what it takes to build and run a screen estate, from the displays through to the support that stays with you. This check asks a different question, and the two do not overlap.

What you get at the end

  • A verdict set by your weakest area, never the average, with all four bands printed so you can see the rule.
  • A count of the screens showing a message nobody chose this month, worked from two figures you give us and nothing else.
  • Every gap quoted back in your own words, matched to the named part of OneRevel accountable for closing it, including one gap that is not theirs to close.
  • A first 90 days in order, starting with the smallest piece of work worth doing, which is how OneRevel's own customers usually start.
